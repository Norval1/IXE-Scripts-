Foundation What Is a Company Structure?
▼
A Pty Ltd company is a separate legal entity. It can own assets, earn income, borrow money, and pay tax — all in its own name. When a company buys a property, the property belongs to the company, not to you personally.

✓ Benefits

  • Franking credits on dividends paid to shareholders
  • Hold money inside the company at lower tax rate
  • Asset protection — personal assets separate from company
  • Serviceability protection — borrowing doesn't show on personal credit
  • Can loan money between entities within the structure

✗ Downsides

  • Higher interest rates — typically 0.5–1.5% above residential
  • Less lenders — mostly non-bank or commercial lending
  • No 50% CGT discount — full capital gain is taxed
  • No negative gearing flowing through to personal income
  • Losses trapped inside company — can't offset your salary
Franking Credits — What They Are
Tax Franking Credits — Simple Explanation
▼
When a company pays tax on its profit, and then distributes that profit to shareholders as a dividend — the shareholders would normally be taxed again on that same money. Franking credits prevent that double taxation.

How it works:
Company earns $100 profit → pays 30% company tax = $30 tax paid → distributes $70 to you as a dividend + attaches a $30 franking credit.

When you lodge your tax return, you declare the full $100 as income but get a $30 tax credit applied. If your personal tax on $100 is $37 — you only pay the extra $7. The company already paid the rest.

If your marginal rate is lower than the company rate — you can actually get a refund of the difference.
Why it matters: If you're retired or on a lower income, franking credits can generate a tax refund. For investors building long-term wealth through a company, franking credits make dividends significantly more tax efficient than taking the money as personal income.
Serviceability Protection — What It Means
Borrowing Serviceability Protection Explained
▼
When you borrow money in your personal name, banks assess your personal debt-to-income ratio. Every loan you take reduces how much you can borrow next time — because your personal serviceability shrinks.

When debt sits inside a company: Banks assess the company separately. The company's loan is against the company's income (rent) and assets (the property). Your personal borrowing capacity can remain largely intact — freeing you to borrow again in your personal name for a new build where you want the tax benefits.

This is how serious investors build large portfolios — they separate entities strategically so no single structure gets maxed out.
How to explain it to a client: "The bank doesn't see you as a person anymore — they see a business. A business that owns a cash-flowing asset, has equity, and is generating income. Age doesn't matter. Salary doesn't matter. They're assessing the entity, not the individual."
Advanced The Holding Company Structure
▼
A holding company sits above all your operating entities. It doesn't trade or take on business risk — it simply holds assets, receives income from the entities below it, and distributes money strategically. The trading activity and the risk stays in separate companies underneath.

What Each Entity Does
Role Holding Company — What It Actually Does
▼
  • 💰 Holds money — profits from the trading company flow up as dividends (no dividend withholding tax between Australian companies). Money sits in the holding company at company tax rate (25–30%) rather than your personal rate.
  • 🏦 Loans money down — holding company can lend money to the investment company at a commercial interest rate. That interest is deductible for the investment company and income for the holding company — all within your structure.
  • 🛡️ Asset protection — valuable assets never sit in the entity that faces business or legal risk. The holding company and investment company are clean.
  • 📊 Franking credits — when the holding company pays dividends to you as shareholder, those dividends carry franking credits for the tax already paid at company level. You avoid being taxed twice on the same dollar.
  • 🔄 Income flexibility — you control when and how much dividend you pay yourself. In a low income year — pay yourself more. In a high income year — leave it in the company at the lower rate.
Loans How the Holding Company Loans Money
▼
The inter-company loan:

Trading company makes profit → pays dividend (franked) up to holding company → holding company now has cash sitting at 30% tax rate.

Instead of paying that cash out to you personally (where it becomes income), the holding company loans it to the investment company to fund a property purchase.

Why this is powerful:
The investment company gets access to capital without going to a bank. The interest paid to the holding company is deductible for the investment company. The holding company receives interest income — all staying within your structure, at company tax rates.
ATO rules: Inter-company loans must be at a commercial arm's length interest rate — you can't lend at 0%. The ATO sets a benchmark rate each year. Get your accountant to document this properly.
Strategy Tenants in Common — What It Is
▼
Tenants in Common means two or more entities own a property together — but each owns a defined, separate share. Unlike joint tenants (where both own 100% together), tenants in common allows you to split ownership in any ratio: 50/50, 60/40, 99/1 — whatever makes the most financial sense.

The key: Each owner's share is treated as their own asset for tax purposes. So a personal name gets personal name tax treatment on their share. A company gets company tax treatment on its share.

Why This Works — The Tax Split
By splitting ownership across two entities, you get the best of both worlds:

• Personal name share — qualifies for negative gearing on new builds, 50% CGT discount, full depreciation. All the immediate tax benefits flow through to reduce your personal taxable income.

• Company share — sits at a lower flat tax rate, asset protected, doesn't affect your personal borrowing capacity. Losses stay in the company and carry forward indefinitely.

Tax CGT & Indexation — Explained Simply
▼
What is Capital Gains Tax?
When you sell an investment property for more than you paid for it — the profit is called a capital gain. The ATO taxes that gain. How much you pay depends on your structure and how long you held the asset.
The Three CGT Methods
Method 1 50% CGT Discount — Personal Name (Most Common)
▼
Who gets it: Individuals and trusts — if the asset is held for 12+ months.

How it works: You only pay tax on 50% of the capital gain. The other half is tax-free.

Example:
Buy for $600K → sell for $900K = $300K gain
50% discount applied → taxed on $150K only
At 37% marginal rate → tax = $55,500

Without the discount you'd pay $111,000. The discount saves you $55,500.
Method 2 Indexation — Unit Trusts (Pre-1999 Assets)
▼
What indexation was: Instead of halving the gain, you adjusted your original purchase price upward to account for inflation before calculating the gain. This reduced the taxable profit in line with how much prices rose generally.

Example from the transcript:
Buy for $1M → sell for $2M = $1M gain on face value
But over 10 years, inflation was 50%
So indexed cost base = $1.5M
Taxable gain = $2M − $1.5M = $500K only

Inside a unit trust you can still access indexation (for assets acquired before 11:45am 21 September 1999). For newer assets, unit trusts can access the 50% discount instead — just like individuals.

Key point from the transcript: "Inside of a unit trust, you do get the indexation — which means if you buy for $1M and sell for $2M, but over 10 years there was 50% inflation, your cost base becomes $1.5M. You only pay tax on the $500K above that."
Method 3 Company — No Discount, No Indexation
▼
Companies get neither the 50% discount nor indexation.

Every dollar of capital gain is taxed at the full company rate — 25% (base rate entities) or 30% (larger companies).

Same example:
Buy for $600K → sell for $900K = $300K gain
No discount → taxed on full $300K
At 25% company rate → tax = $75,000

Compare to personal name: $55,500 with the 50% discount

So in this example the company pays $19,500 MORE in CGT — even though the tax rate is lower.

This is why companies are typically better for buy-and-hold strategies where you're not planning to sell — and personal name is better when eventual capital gains are large.

Case Study How a Company Structure Unlocks Borrowing Capacity
▼
This is a real-world example of how repositioning an existing property into a company structure can completely change what the bank will lend — without changing your income, your deposit, or the property itself.
The 5-Step Journey
1
What You've Already Got
You own a property worth $1M. It's positively cash-flowed — meaning the rent coming in is greater than the holding costs. That property is paying for itself and putting money in your pocket. That's the foundation.
2
The Structure Shift
Instead of owning that property in your personal name, we put it inside a company structure. Your company. So now the company owns the asset — not you personally. The property hasn't changed. The tenant hasn't changed. But the ownership entity has.
3
What the Bank Now Sees
When the bank looks at your company's loan application, they see a completely different picture. They see a business that owns a $1M asset, that is cash-flow positive, that has equity. Suddenly — age doesn't matter. Whether you're single or partnered doesn't matter. They're lending to a business, not a person. The assessment criteria shifts entirely.
4
New Borrowing Power
In personal name, borrowing capacity might be capped at $400K. Inside the company structure — looking at $950K to $1M in borrowing capacity. That's an extra $550–600K that simply didn't exist before. Same person. Same property. Different structure.
5
What You Do With It
Take that extra $550–600K and buy the next property. Same process — positive cash flow target, company owns it. As the portfolio grows and properties become self-funding, the company's ability to borrow again resets. Rinse and repeat. This is the compounding engine.
