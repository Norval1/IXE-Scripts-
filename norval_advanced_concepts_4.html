The property's VALUE goes up over time.

Why Growth Wins First
"When you're building a portfolio, the key ingredient is leverage. Leverage comes from capital growth — not yield. If your property goes up $400K in 5 years, you can release that equity and buy the next one. If you've got a property putting $20 a week in your pocket but going nowhere in value, you're stuck. A static portfolio doesn't grow. You need the springboard property first — high growth, right location — and that becomes the engine that funds the next purchase. Then once you have two or three going, THAT'S when you layer in higher yielding assets to balance cash flow."

Gross vs Net Yield
Gross Yield

Annual rent ÷ Purchase price × 100

$30,000 ÷ $700,000 = 4.3% gross

What gets advertised. BEFORE all expenses.
Net Yield

(Rent minus ALL expenses) ÷ Price × 100

Expenses: rates, insurance, management, maintenance

What actually goes in your pocket. Always 20–25% less than gross.
The rule: Commercial triple net = yield you see IS net yield. Residential — always assume 20–25% of gross rent disappears in expenses. A 6% gross residential yield is realistically 4.5% net.

Retirement phase — when you need income NOW, not growth later.
Large portfolio (3–4 properties) — once growth properties are compounding, add a high-yielding asset for cash flow balance.
Neutral gearing goal — some clients want the property to pay for itself rather than cost them weekly — valid strategy for later stage.

The Depreciation Law — Why First Owner Wins
"Depreciation is not about the value of the property — it's about the LAWS. The law says: the maximum depreciation goes to the FIRST owner of a brand new property. Even if I bought a property and sold it to you before construction finished — you're still not the first owner legally. I was. The depreciation entitlement was mine. Second owner, third owner — the laws don't favour them. This is why builders don't build houses and sit on them hoping someone buys. They build for buyers who've already committed. And that buyer — if it's you in your own name — gets every dollar of that depreciation. It directly reduces your taxable income for the first 7–10 years."
Objection — "Established Is Cheaper"
PROSPECT: "I can get an established property cheaper. Why pay more for new?"
"Good question — and price matters. But let's look at total cost. With established: full stamp duty, zero depreciation, zero negative gearing under the new rules, plus maintenance spending from day one.

With new: stamp duty on land only, maximum depreciation for 7+ years, full negative gearing, builder's warranty. That depreciation alone — on a 4-bed house and land — can be $15–20K in year one. At a 37% tax bracket that's a $7,000 tax refund. Over 7 years that's $40,000+ the tax office gives you back. Does established still look cheaper when you factor that in?"
The "12 Months Old = New" Myth
"Builders don't build houses and sit on them hoping someone buys. That doesn't happen. If a property is 12 months old and no one's bought it — there's a reason. And even if you found one — the first owner took the depreciation. You'd get the scraps. New build means YOU are the first owner. That's the only way to get the full depreciation schedule working for you."
Tax BenefitDepreciation — How It Works in Australia
▼
Simple Explanation
"Depreciation is the ATO acknowledging that a building and everything inside it wears out over time. As the property owner, you can claim that wear and tear as a tax deduction every year — even though you're not spending any money. It's a paper loss that reduces your taxable income without costing you anything out of pocket."
Two Types
Division 43 — Building Write-off

The structure itself: walls, floors, roof.

Claimed at 2.5% of construction cost per year for 40 years.

$450K build cost = $11,250/year for 40 years.
Division 40 — Plant & Equipment

Everything inside: appliances, carpet, hot water, blinds, A/C.

Claimed at higher rates — faster deductions in early years.

New build: ~$8–15K additional per year in early years.

PAYG Variation — Get It Weekly, Not Annually
What it is: Instead of waiting for your annual tax return, you lodge a PAYG Variation with the ATO. Your employer adjusts withholding tax WEEKLY — you see the benefit in every pay cycle.

What you need: A quantity surveyor depreciation schedule (~$700, one-off, tax deductible). Submit to accountant → they lodge the PAYG variation → weekly pay increases.

Tell clients: "Instead of the government holding your money all year and giving it back in July, you get it in your pay every fortnight. That's the money that makes the property cash flow neutral or even positive."

Over 10 years — that's $60,000+ in tax benefits you're leaving on the table by buying established. Factor that in and tell me which one's actually cheaper."

Borrowing as Tax-Free Money
"Here's something most people never understand. When your property goes up $300,000 in value, you can release that equity as cash. You borrow against it. That borrowing is NOT income — it's not taxable. You receive $300K in your bank account and the ATO doesn't touch a cent of it. You use that as a deposit on the next property. The Wealth Loop
"You need a vehicle to make cash flow money. Without income you ain't building a portfolio. But without property, your income stops the day you stop working. The job makes the cash. The cash buys the property. The property grows. The equity buys more property. The tenant services the debt. You don't sell — you leverage. That's how anyone who's built a large portfolio has done it."

Never Paying Off Investment Debt
"The whole concept of paying off your investment property debt is counter-productive. Every dollar you pay off the principal is a dollar you can't borrow again. The goal isn't to own the property outright — the goal is to have rent service the loan, the property growing in value, and your equity available to deploy into the next purchase. The debt doesn't go away — it gets transferred to better and better assets. You're not paying off debt; you're moving it up the asset ladder."
Loan StructureInterest Only vs Principal & Interest — When and Why
▼

✓ Interest Only (IO) — Investment

  • Pay interest only — never touch principal
  • Lower monthly repayments
  • 100% of repayment is tax deductible
  • Preserves cash flow for holding costs
  • Frees capital for next deposit
  • Best for: investment in growth phase

✗ P&I — Your Home Loan

  • Higher repayments — paying principal down
  • Deductible interest reduces each year
  • Builds equity faster but uses more cash
  • Best for: owner-occupied — non-deductible debt
  • Goal: clear this one FAST
Why IO on Investment
"On an investment property we use interest-only for two reasons. First — lower repayments means better cash flow and the property is easier to hold. Second — 100% of your repayment is interest, and 100% of that interest is tax deductible. The moment you start paying principal, you're using after-tax dollars to reduce a deductible debt. That's inefficient. Direct every extra dollar into your home loan offset — where the benefit is dollar for dollar — or into your next deposit."
The Simple Explanation
"Two properties — your home and your investment. Your home loan is after-tax money and you want to smash it down. Your investment — the tenant is helping pay it and the interest is a deduction. Keep the investment on interest only, directing those monthly savings into your home offset. You pay off your home faster. The investment looks after itself. Once the home's clear, you redirect everything into building the investment portfolio."

StrategyFamily Pledge / Security Guarantee — How It Works
▼
What Is It?
A Family Pledge (or Family Security Guarantee) allows a family member — typically a parent — to use equity in their own property as additional security for your loan. This means you can borrow with a smaller deposit (or even zero deposit) without paying Lender's Mortgage Insurance (LMI).

The parent doesn't give money. They don't make repayments. They pledge their equity as security — it's only called upon if you default.
How It Works
Family Pledge Structure
You (Buyer) borrows 95–100% of property value
Mum & Dad (Guarantors) → pledge equity in their home → Bank
Bank uses parent's equity to cover the shortfall between your deposit and the 80% LVR threshold. When your property grows to 80% LVR — release the guarantee. Parents' home is no longer at risk.

Risk — Parents Must Understand: If the borrower defaults, the bank can pursue the guarantor's property to recover the shortfall. This is real — not a formality. Best practice: only pledge for the gap amount (not the whole loan). Release the guarantee as soon as the property reaches 80% LVR.
How to Explain It
"A family pledge is basically Mum and Dad saying — 'We'll back you to the bank with the equity in our home.' They don't give you money. They don't make repayments. They just lend their equity as extra security so the bank says yes without requiring a 20% deposit from you. The moment your property grows enough that you're at 80% of its value — you release the guarantee. Mum and Dad are out of the picture. Most people use this to get in the market 2–3 years earlier than they otherwise would."
