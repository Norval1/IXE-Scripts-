Core ConceptCapital Growth vs Rental Yield — What Actually Makes You Wealthy
▼
The Difference — Simple Version
Capital Growth

The property's VALUE goes up over time.

Example: Buy $700K → worth $1.1M in 6 years = $400K gain

This is how you build NET WORTH and leverage into the next property.
Rental Yield

How much RENT you collect vs the property price.

Example: $30K rent ÷ $700K value = 4.3% yield

This is your CASH FLOW — what hits your pocket each week.
Why Growth Wins at the Start — Kieran's Framework
"When you're building a portfolio, the key ingredient is leverage. Leverage comes from capital growth — not yield. If your property goes up $400K in 5 years, you can release that equity and buy the next one. If you've got a property putting $20 a week in your pocket but going nowhere in value, you're stuck. A static portfolio doesn't grow. You need the springboard property first — high growth, right location — and that becomes the engine that funds the next purchase. Then once you have two or three going, THAT'S when you layer in higher yielding assets to balance cash flow."

Gross Yield vs Net Yield
Gross Yield

Annual rent ÷ Purchase price × 100

$30,000 rent ÷ $700,000 = 4.3% gross

This is what gets advertised. BEFORE expenses.
Net Yield

(Rent minus ALL expenses) ÷ Purchase price × 100

Expenses: rates, insurance, management, maintenance, body corp

Net is what actually goes in your pocket. Always lower than gross.
Jack Henderson's rule: Commercial triple net = the yield you see IS the net yield. Residential — always assume 20–25% of gross rent disappears in expenses. A 6% gross residential yield is realistically 4.5% net.

The Depreciation Advantage — Kieran's Lesson
"Depreciation is not about the value of the property — it's about the LAWS. The law says: the maximum depreciation goes to the FIRST owner of a brand new property. Even if I bought a property and sold it to you before construction finished — you're still not the first owner legally. I was. The depreciation entitlement was mine. Second owner, third owner — the laws don't favour them. This is why builders don't just build houses and sit on them hoping someone buys. They build for buyers who've already committed. And that buyer — if it's you in your own name — gets every dollar of that depreciation. It directly reduces your taxable income for the first 7–10 years."
The "Just Buy Established It's Cheaper" Objection
PROSPECT: "I can get an established property cheaper. Why would I pay more for new?"
"Good question — and price matters. But let's look at total cost. With established: full stamp duty on the purchase price, zero depreciation, zero negative gearing under the new rules, plus you'll be spending on maintenance from day one.

With new: stamp duty on land only, maximum depreciation for 7+ years, full negative gearing, builder's warranty. That depreciation alone — on a 4-bed house and land — can be $15–20K in year one. At a 37% tax bracket that's a $7,000 tax refund. Over 7 years that's $40,000+ the tax office effectively gives you back. Does established still look cheaper when you factor that in?"
Why People Think "12 Months Old = New"
Kieran's lesson: "Builders don't build houses and sit on them hoping someone buys. That's nonsense. If a property is 12 months old and no one's bought it — something's wrong. And even if you found one — the first owner took the depreciation. You'd get the scraps. New build means YOU are the first owner. That's the only way to get the full depreciation schedule working for you."
Tax BenefitDepreciation — How It Works in Australia
▼
What Is It?
"Depreciation is the ATO acknowledging that a building and everything inside it wears out over time. As the property owner, you can claim that 'wear and tear' as a tax deduction every year — even though you're not spending any money. It's a paper loss that reduces your taxable income without costing you anything out of pocket."
Two Types of Depreciation
Division 43 — Building Write-off

The structure itself: walls, floors, roof.

Claimed at 2.5% of construction cost per year for 40 years.

$250K build cost = $6,250/year in deductions for 40 years.
Division 40 — Plant & Equipment

Everything inside: appliances, carpet, hot water system, blinds, A/C.

Claimed at higher rates (Diminishing Value method = faster deductions in early years).

New build: ~$10–15K additional per year in early years.

PAYG Variation — Get It Weekly
What it is: Instead of waiting for your annual tax return, you lodge a PAYG Variation form with the ATO. Your employer adjusts your withholding tax WEEKLY — so you see the benefit in every pay cycle, not once a year.

What you need: A quantity surveyor to produce a depreciation schedule ($600–800, one-off cost, tax deductible). Submit the schedule to your accountant → they lodge the PAYG variation → weekly pay goes up.

For clients: "Instead of the government holding your money all year and giving it back in July, you get it in your pay every fortnight. That's the money that makes the property cash flow neutral or positive."

Cross-Securitisation Warning — Jack Henderson
NEVER do this: Don't use property 1 as security for property 2's loan. If property 1's value drops, the bank can restrict access to property 2's equity. You lose control.

DO this instead: Standalone equity release from property 1 → cash sits in your offset → you use that cash as a clean deposit for property 2 independently.

Jack: "The bank will tell you what you can and can't do. If the value on one property falls, they will NOT allow you to access equity out of the other because they're tied together. Always do a clean equity release first."
Never Having to Pay Off Your Debt
"The whole concept of paying off your investment property debt is actually counter-productive. Every dollar you pay off the principal is a dollar you can't borrow again. The goal isn't to own the property outright — the goal is to have the property's rent service the loan, the property growing in value, and your equity available to deploy into the next purchase. The debt doesn't go away — it gets transferred to better and better assets. You're not paying off debt; you're moving it up the asset ladder."
Loan StructureInterest Only vs Principal & Interest — When and Why
▼
Interest Only (IO)

You only pay the interest — never touch the principal.

Monthly repayments are LOWER.

Deductible portion stays HIGH (100% interest).

Preserves cash flow and borrowing capacity.

Best for: investment properties in growth phase
Principal & Interest (P&I)

Each payment covers interest PLUS reduces the loan balance.

Higher repayments but building equity faster.

Deductible amount decreases as principal reduces.

Best for: owner-occupied home, debt you want gone
Why IO on Investment Properties
"On an investment property, we use interest-only loans for two reasons. First — lower repayments means better cash flow, which means the property is easier to hold and service from day one. Second — 100% of your repayment is the interest, and 100% of that interest is tax deductible. The moment you start paying principal, you're using after-tax dollars to reduce a deductible debt. That's inefficient. You want to direct every extra dollar either into your offset account on your home loan — where the benefit is dollar for dollar — or into your next deposit."
Effect on Borrowing Capacity
Important: Banks assess your borrowing capacity based on P&I repayments — even if you're on IO. They use a 'stress test rate' (typically 2–3% above current rates) and assume P&I from day one. So IO doesn't give you more borrowing capacity in the bank's eyes — but it does reduce your ACTUAL monthly outgoings, which gives you more cash in hand to service additional properties.

Key: When your IO period ends (typically 5 years), the loan switches to P&I and repayments jump significantly. Plan for this.
The Simple Explanation for Clients
"Think of it this way. You've got two properties — your home and your investment. Your home loan is costing you after-tax money and you want to smash the debt down. Your investment property — the tenant is helping pay it and the interest is a tax deduction. So you keep the investment on interest only, directing those savings into your home loan offset. You pay off your home faster. The investment looks after itself. Once the home's done, you redirect everything into building equity in the investments."

Jack's Simple Explanation of Trusts
"A trust is just a structure where one person — the trustee — controls assets that belong to the trust, for the benefit of the beneficiaries. The magic is this: at the end of each year, the trustee decides who gets the income. You've got a family with four incomes at different tax rates — you distribute to whoever pays the least tax. StrategyFamily Pledge / Security Guarantee — How It Works
▼
What Is It?
A Family Pledge (or Family Security Guarantee) allows a family member — typically a parent — to use equity in their own property as additional security for your loan. This means you can borrow with a smaller deposit (or even zero deposit) without paying Lender's Mortgage Insurance (LMI).

The parent doesn't give money — they pledge their equity as security. They don't make repayments. It's only called upon if you default.
How It Works Visually
Family Pledge Structure
You (Buyer) borrows 95–100% of property value
Mum & Dad (Guarantors) → pledge equity in their home → Bank (Security)
Bank uses parent's property equity to cover the shortfall between your deposit and the 80% LVR threshold. When your property grows in value to 80% LVR — release the guarantee. Parents' home is no longer at risk.
The Numbers
Example: $600K Property
Without Family PledgeWith Family Pledge

The Risk — What Parents Need to Know
If the borrower defaults: The bank can pursue the guarantor's property to recover the shortfall. This is real risk — not a formality.

Best practice: Only pledge for the gap amount (not the whole loan). Release the guarantee as soon as possible once the property grows or the loan reduces to 80% LVR.
How to Explain It to Clients
"A family pledge is basically Mum and Dad saying — 'We'll back you to the bank with the equity we have in our home.' They don't give you money. They don't make repayments. They just lend their equity as extra security so the bank says yes without requiring a 20% deposit from you. The moment your property grows enough that you're at 80% of its value — you release the guarantee. Mum and Dad are no longer in the picture. Most people use this to get in the market 2–3 years earlier than they otherwise would."
