Col's strategy session frameworks — condensed in your style. Use these as pre-objection handling and education drops.
Station 1 Can We Do This Logistically?
▼
The Two Sides: Deposit funding on the left — cash, usable equity, defence entitlements, super. Borrowing capacity on the right. No use having one without the other.
Investment vs Owner-Occupier Lending: For a home to live in, banks assess surplus income only. For an investment property, they also count the property's future rental income — which means your investment borrowing capacity is often higher than your home-buyer borrowing capacity. Say this explicitly.
Equity Caveat: Equity is only usable if the person who can borrow against it is on the title, and the property can service more debt. Stuck equity = liquidate and redeploy as cash.
Defence Entitlements — Don't Cash Early: DHA subsidised rent is worth roughly $300/week ($15,600/year). A $30K first home owner grant is paid back in about two years of continued service. Don't trade an ongoing benefit for a one-off number.
Station 2 Is Now a Good Time?
▼
Bonds vs Shares vs Property — Same $200K:
• $200K in bonds at 10% = $20K growth
• $200K as deposit on $800K property at 10% = $80K growth
Same money, same return rate — but 4x more in property because of leverage. Let the arithmetic make the point, not opinion.
Offset Account: Money in an offset effectively earns you your mortgage rate (say 6.5%) completely tax-free. To earn the same spendable outcome from shares, you'd need a gross return of 10–11% before tax. The comparison isn't 6.5% vs 6.5% — it's 6.5% tax-free vs 10–11% taxed.
Budget / Negative Gearing: If you disincentivise investors from owning rental stock, you decrease rental supply without changing rental demand — so rents go up. This happened in Australia in the 1980s and reversed within two years. Same thing in New Zealand. Historical proof of concept, not opinion. Today's vacancy rates (0.3–1.8%) are half what they were in the 80s — the same policy causes more damage now.
Apple Tree Analogy: Funnelling investors toward new construction only doesn't create more homes — it just adds more competition for the same limited build volume. If the tree only grows 5 apples a year and you double demand, you still only get 5 apples.
Station 3 New vs Established
▼
Definition first: "Established" is a legal/tax classification, not an age description. A brand-new home someone else built and sold to you is considered established by the ATO.
Stamp Duty Gap: New build — duty on land component only (~$15K on a $1M property). Established — duty on full price (~$45–55K). That gap alone is $30–40K before you've even compared anything else.
Depreciation Gap — the big one:
• Established: prime cost method, structure only, flat 2.5% over 40 years
• New build: diminishing value method, structure AND fixtures/fittings, front-loaded in first 5–7 years
• Under 2026 budget rules, established properties get zero negative gearing benefit at all
• New build can return $11K+ tax return vs near-zero for established on identical properties
• Over 7 years, that's $100K+ more in deductions from a new build
Structural Guarantee: New build inspected and signed off at slab, frame, and enclosed — with photos and certification. Established: zero structural guarantee or warranty from vendor. Car warranty analogy — would you buy the 1-year or 10-year warranty?
Holding Costs vs Stamp Duty: Construction period costs (~$30K, tax-deductible) are less than established stamp duty alone (~$40K+, not deductible) — before the depreciation gap is even counted.
Station 4 Equilibrium Point Theory
▼
The Core Idea: Property investing is a science, not an art. There's a mathematical sweet spot — an equilibrium point — where the price, land size, and specification align with what the actual owner-occupier market in that location can afford and wants to buy.
You're building for the buyer, not yourself: You're building the house the largest pool of future owner-occupier buyers in that suburb can afford and would want — because they drive your capital growth and resale value.
Don't overcapitalise:
• Biggest land on the street = odd one out, smaller resale pool
• 5 bedrooms in a young-couple suburb = wrong demographic
• Pool = half your buyers see it as a liability, not a bonus
More spend is not automatically more growth — it can actually shrink your buyer pool at resale.
Locations Why We Buy Where We Buy
▼
Urban Growth Boundary: Every capital city has a planning boundary past which land can't be rezoned. Growth gets pushed to satellite corridors sitting inside or just outside it — and those corridors fill in over 15–20 years as infrastructure and population catch up.
The Investment Thesis: Identify a growth corridor while land is comparatively cheap and infrastructure is still being built — then let population growth, employment, and transport investment close the price gap to established suburbs nearer the CBD. That gap closing IS the growth.
Don't sell on vibes: Show specifically funded, approved infrastructure and population trajectory. Frame the current price gap to a comparable established suburb as the growth still left to capture. That's what makes it science, not a guess.
PPOR vs IP Investment First — Why It Makes Sense
▼
PPOR = lifestyle decision wearing a financial costume. Investment property = financial decision that might become a lifestyle decision later, once it's done its job.
The Sequencing Argument: Investment property first = higher borrowing capacity (rental income counted) + tax deductions working for you. A home first = no deductions, lower borrowing capacity for everything that follows.
Nothing stops you from turning an investment property into your home later — once it's paid down and worked for you first.
Col Style Col's Delivery Habits to Steal
▼
The Train Track: Narrate the shape of the conversation as you go — "So that's the first station on the train track... the next station is..." Keeps the client oriented and makes it feel like a deliberate methodology.
The Verdict Habit: Ask the station question plainly, answer in one word, pause, then explain. "Can you do this logistically? Yes." The pause does the work.
Analogy First: Every abstract concept gets a plain analogy before the numbers — apple tree (supply), car warranty (build quality), offset (tax-free savings). Analogy → numbers → verdict.
Pre-empt Objections: State the objection yourself before the client raises it — then answer with a worked number or historical precedent. Never a vague reassurance.
Always Close with One Concrete Next Step: Never end on "let's stay in touch." Name the exact next action and when it happens.
