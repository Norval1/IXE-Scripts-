Worried about getting declined? Read this first

Short answer: nobody outside the lender can say why Nash was declined three years ago — only Amex has that. What we can say is how this works, and that cancelling a card isn't what triggers a decline.

  • Applying is what leaves a mark. Every application puts a hard enquiry on your credit file for 5 years, approved or not — so Nash's old one is likely still there. Closing a card doesn't add an enquiry; the closed account just stays visible for about 2 years.
  • Common reasons people are declined: too many recent applications, existing card limits that are large against income, income that can't be verified or doesn't match the application, short or patchy credit history, errors on the credit file. Amex members also report letters citing "credit rating and other factors". These are community reports, not Amex's published rules.
  • Find the real reason: look for any decline letter from Amex, ask Amex what the reasons were, and get your credit report from Equifax, Experian and illion (once every 3 months at no charge; checking your own file doesn't affect your score).
  • Safer order: apply one person at a time, spaced out — one or two enquiries a year is normal, five or more in 12 months looks risky. Keep the ANZ cards until the new cards are approved and the bonus spend is done. Check what ANZ does with your points before closing.
  • Shay: Amex excludes the sign-up bonus for anyone who has held an Amex card in the last 18 months, so apply for the business card before any personal Amex. Both of you already hold ANZ Rewards cards, so ANZ bonuses may be off the table.
  • If Amex says no again: the non-Amex cards in the list use different assessments, but each application is another enquiry — don't apply for all of them.

Nobody can promise approval — it's the lender's decision. General information only; not credit or financial advice.
