Market Market Cycle + Opportunity Cost
▼
Here's the thing — when you go back and look at 40 years of property data, prices have never fallen two years running. 2018 — down one year, up three. 2022 — down one year, up three. Now we're in 2026, same pattern. Prices are down, but history says they rebound from 2027.

Every time prices have fallen, government has stepped in — rate hikes, budget changes, lending restrictions. But here's what they haven't fixed — the real problem. All that intervention does is create a cycle. Sentiment shifts temporarily, but the fundamentals? They don't change.
Data The Real Problem — Supply vs Demand
▼
Last year we needed to build more than 250,000 homes just to keep up with population growth and start closing the shortage. We only started construction on 196,000. So we're already behind.

But it gets worse. We're short 83,000 skilled tradies right now to even build what we're currently trying to build. We need another 116,000 construction workers by 2029. And meanwhile? Australia is bringing in 300,000-plus people through net migration every year — all of those people need housing.

So you've got massive demand — 300,000 new Australians a year — and falling supply. We're not building enough homes. We don't have the tradies to build them. That's not sentiment, that's not government policy — that's a structural gap that doesn't close overnight.

The cost of delaying isn't zero. It's exponentially higher than just moving now. And given that supply keeps tightening, the prices available today are only going to look more attractive in a few years' time when demand has caught up again.
